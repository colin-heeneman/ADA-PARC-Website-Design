Private Health Coverage

Working-Age People With Disabilities Covered by Private, Usually Employer, Insurance

Population covered: The civilian noninstitutionalized population ages 19 to 64. Private and public coverage are not mutually exclusive.

Employer-based private insurance reaches far fewer working-age adults with disabilities than other adults.1 A low private-coverage share can reflect weak access to benefit-carrying jobs, or public programmes doing that job instead, so this sheet ranks states only by size.

Why This Matters

Private insurance, most often through an employer, covers about 49 percent of working-age people with disabilities, against roughly 76 percent of people without.1

As with public coverage, a private-coverage share reads two ways: a high figure may reflect good access to jobs that carry benefits, and a low figure may reflect public programmes successfully filling the gap. This sheet is shaded by size, not by performance.

People can hold private and public coverage at the same time, so this measure and the public-coverage sheet are not mutually exclusive.2

What This Map Measures

The maps show the share of working-age people with and without disabilities who have private insurance, most often through an employer, on one shared scale. The four bands are labelled by size rather than Excellent to Poor, because a high or low figure can each be read two ways.
