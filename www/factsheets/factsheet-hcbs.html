State Spending on Community Living
for People with Disabilities

Printed by ADA-PARC, September 2025

ada

The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) mandates the right to live in the community with supports instead of living in a nursing home or long-term care institution. This right was upheld by the U.S. Supreme Court in the landmark Olmstead decision in 1999.1

Despite this right to live in the community, some states invest more than others in each person they serve. The map and tables below display state Medicaid spending per recipient on home- and community-based services (HCBS), inclusive of all waiver types (Community First Choice, Section 1915(i), Section 1915(c), Section 1515), for people with disabilities (all ages) living in the community. This is the total HCBS expenditure divided by the number of people receiving HCBS.2

Lower spending per recipient means fewer financial resources reaching each person with a disability who relies on community services. Higher spending per recipient means stronger financial support for each person's community living. This measure reflects how generously a state supports each recipient, not how many people it reaches. Rebalancing money toward community living remains an ADA-related disparity issue for many people with disabilities, putting them at higher risk for institutionalization. Currently, this community living support is highly dependent on the zip code and state in which you live.

State Map

How to read the colors. States are shaded by performance tier, not by raw dollars. Darker maroon marks the most favorable outcomes (Excellent), here the highest community living spending per recipient. Lighter gold marks the least favorable outcomes (Poor), the lowest spending per recipient.
Who receives HCBS. Among people receiving HCBS nationally, recipients span multiple disability groups: about 42% have mental health conditions or substance use disorders, 24% have autism, intellectual, or developmental disabilities, 11% are adults under 65 with other potentially disabling conditions, and 20% are older adults. People can belong to more than one group, so shares add to more than 100%.

Community vs. Institutional Spending

Spending per recipient shows how much reaches each person, but not how a state divides its long-term care budget between community and institutional settings. This second measure shows the share of each state’s Medicaid long-term services and supports (LTSS) spending that goes to home- and community-based services (HCBS) rather than to institutions such as nursing facilities and intermediate care facilities. A higher share means a state directs more of its long-term care dollars toward community living. A lower share means more of its dollars go to institutional care.3

How to read the colors. States are shaded by performance tier, not by raw share. Darker maroon marks the most favorable outcomes (Excellent), here the highest community (HCBS) share of long-term care spending. Lighter gold marks the least favorable outcomes (Poor), where more spending goes to institutions.

About These Data

The information shown on this website comes from research conducted by Mathematica. We did not collect or create the original data. We organized and presented the information to make it easier for community members, including people with disabilities, to understand and compare. All credit for the original research, data collection, and analysis belongs to Mathematica and the report’s authors. Any changes we made were limited to how the information is displayed and did not alter the original data.

  1. Office for Civil Rights. (2018, June 28). Serving people with disabilities in the most integrated setting: Community living and Olmstead. U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.
  2. Mathematica, prepared for the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). Medicaid LTSS Users & Expenditures, 2023 Data Tables (T-MSIS Analytic Files). Spending per recipient = total HCBS expenditure (A2_LTSSExpDlvrySystm_2023.xlsx, tab “A.2.3 All-HCBS”, column “HCBS (total)”) divided by HCBS recipients (A1_LTSSUsrDlvrySystm_2023.xlsx, tab “A.1.3 All-HCBS”, column “HCBS (total)”); both inclusive of fee-for-service and managed care. Subpopulation context from C1_LTSSUsrChar_2023.xlsx, tab “C.1.21 HCBSBySubpop.” See CMS LTSS Reports & Evaluations.
  3. Carpenter, A., Stepanczuk, C., Murray, C., & Wysocki, A. (2025, October 17). Trends in users and expenditures for home and community-based services as a share of total Medicaid long-term services and supports users and expenditures, 2023. Mathematica. Report PDF. Community share of LTSS spending = HCBS expenditure (tab “A.2.3 All-HCBS,” column “HCBS (total)”) divided by the sum of HCBS and institutional expenditure (tab “A.2.2 All-Inst,” column “Institutional (total)”) from A2_LTSSExpDlvrySystm_2023.xlsx. Because LTSS equals HCBS plus institutional spending, this equals HCBS as a share of total Medicaid LTSS expenditure.
